## Core difficulty

The receive side already exists (splat PS does 3x3 PCF against the single player-anchored cascade), so the real work is a new depth-only caster for the chunked splat terrain into the DiligentFX cascaded depth atlas, with the tricky parts being (a) the anchor-space/light-space matrix conventions across three different consumers (runtime-HLSL transposed cbuffers vs DiligentFX row-major math), and (b) a bias policy that stays self-consistent between the new caster and the existing receiver-side NDC bias.

## Reductions / key lemmas

1. **The caster needs no new shader and no new vertex data.** The splat VS (`materials/splat_terrain_vs.hlsl`) computes `clip = mul(mul(pos - g_Anchor, cCamView), cCamProj)` — a pure two-matrix projection. The cascade's `WorldToLightProjSpace` (row-major math, untransposed) maps *anchored* space to light clip space (proven by the receiver: `ShadowPos = mul(float4(In.AnchoredPos,1), sWorldToLightProj)` in `splat_terrain_ps.hlsl:416`, and by the gltf caster writing the light view-proj into the PBR VS's camera slot). So the caster = existing splat VS + a depth-only PSO with `cCamView = transpose(W2L)`, `cCamProj = identity`, `g_Anchor = camera anchor`, reusing each chunk's vbo/ibo verbatim. No PS at all (Diligent depth-only pipeline, 0 color RTs — unlike `gltfDiligentShadowDraw`, which needs three throwaway color RTs because its PBR PSO is 3-RT).

2. **Per-cascade culling is free with existing code.** `frustumCullFromVP(vp, glNdc=false, planes)` in `FrustumCull.h` builds frustum planes from a row-major Diligent `view*proj`; a cascade's `WorldToLightProjSpace` is exactly that form in anchored space (the distribution padding is already baked into the transform — no need to know `casterPad`). Test `aabb ± anchor` boxes with `frustumCullAabbOutside`, exactly as the world draw does, but skip the 1-chunk camera window (chunks beyond the camera must still cast into the cascade). 2–3 cascades × a handful of chunks each → negligible CPU.

3. **Bias convention must match, or acne/peter-panning.** The module header states the contract: "casters draw without a rasterizer bias, so the receiver must subtract it" — and `shadowDiligentCasterBias()` is currently *unused* by any caster. The splat receiver subtracts `sSlice.y` (= `sm.Padding0` = `shadowDiligentPbrDepthBias()`, `fFixedDepthBias` = 0.0025–0.005 × cascade-0 z-scale, env-tunable via `ENGINE_PBR_BIAS_SCALE`). If the new terrain caster additionally applies a rasterizer bias, it double-counts against the receiver-side subtraction → peter-panning. Decision: the terrain caster draws WITHOUT a rasterizer bias (matching the player caster), keeping the existing receiver compensation valid; once terrain self-shadows, tune the receiver bias via `ENGINE_PBR_BIAS_SCALE` (note the plan's "use `shadowDiligentCasterBias` in the new PSO" would break this — pick one side, not both).

4. **Receive quality is bounded by the single-cascade choice.** The splat receiver only samples the one cascade covering the player's feet +1 m torso (`shadowDiligentPbrWorldToLightProj/Slice/DepthBias`), so terrain outside that cascade's frustum compares against clamped edge depths (sampler is CLAMP) → mostly-lit garbage or a hard edge at the cascade boundary; there is no distance fade in the splat PS. For this task that is acceptable (tier distance 60–120 m covers the visible neighborhood, and the player's shadow lands on nearby terrain, which is the visible case); per-pixel cascade pick is a later extension.

5. **Gating/ordering constraints.** The shadow pass (`renderCascadesImpl` in `ShadowDiligent.cpp:335`) runs *before* `worldDraw`; the splat pass lazily inits inside `splatTerrainDrawDiligent` during the world draw, and the world only draws splat when the gate (`ENGINE_SPLAT_TERRAIN != 0`), terrain is loaded, `taaColorRTV()` exists, and the pass init succeeded. The caster must (a) lazily create its own PSO/SRB on first shadow draw (device exists by then — the shadow pass already allocates textures), (b) be a no-op under the same conditions the world draw uses so casters mirror what is actually rendered, and (c) bind `SetRenderTargets(0, nullptr, dsv, TRANSITION)` itself, because after `gltfDiligentShadowDraw` three dummy color RTVs are still bound (binding extra RTs than a depth-only PSO declares is a validation hazard).

## Candidate approaches

**A. Depth-only caster reusing the splat VS + existing chunk buffers.** Sketch: `splatTerrainShadowDrawDiligent(ctx, cascadeIdx, dsv)` in `SplatTerrainDiligent.cpp` — lazy depth-only PSO (splat VS, D32 only, no rasterizer bias, no cull-mode change), its own shadow-frame staging/cbuffer (same `SplatFrameStaging` shape, per-cascade `cCamView=transpose(W2L)`, `cCamProj=I`, anchor), per-cascade cascade-frustum culling, then a one-line hook in `renderCascadesImpl` after `gltfDiligentShadowDraw`. Risk: getting the transposed/anchored convention wrong produces a mirror/shifted shadow (check vs the player shadow's ground contact first); the `glNdc=false` assumption that the light projection is Vulkan z 0..1 must hold (frustum near/far planes otherwise wrong — readback will reveal it). Effort: ~1–2 focused dev sessions; this is the bulk of the task.

**B. New minimal shadow VS in a new pak shader file.** Sketch: a 20-line VS (position + anchor + one matrix) loaded via `createSplatHlsl`. Risk: requires repacking `pak_1` (the runtime loads shader text from the pak, `utils::dataManagerRead`), adds an asset-maintenance path, and buys almost nothing over A (the world VS's extra register work is irrelevant in a depth pass). Effort: more than A, strictly. Not worth it.

**C. Extend the receiver to per-pixel cascade selection (like the glslang world shaders) + fade to lit.** Sketch: feed all cascade matrices into the splat cbuffer, pick cascade in PS, cross-fade. Risk: cbuffer size/layout growth across the byte-mirror struct, cross-cascade fade math, and it's not required for "terrain receives and casts" — it only fixes distant-terrain receive quality. Effort: large; do after A is visually verified.

## Recommended approach

**A**, with C parked as follow-up. It needs no shader changes, no pak repack, no new vertex data, and every convention it depends on is already proven by existing code (the splat receiver's `AnchoredPos × W2L` and the gltf caster's light-matrix-in-camera-slot both validate the anchor-space light-matrix lemma). For it to work: the cascade `WorldToLightProjSpace` must be in anchored space and Vulkan z 0..1 (verify with a screenshot — the terrain's shadow of a chunk should land where the sun says, matching the player's shadow), and the caster must skip the rasterizer bias to keep the receiver-side `pbrBias` compensation consistent.

## Proposed tasks

1. **Implement the terrain caster**: `splatTerrainShadowDrawDiligent(ctx, cascadeWorldToLightProj, dsv)` in `SplatTerrainDiligent.cpp` — lazy depth-only PSO (splat VS, D32, no rasterizer bias, `SetRenderTargets(0, nullptr, dsv)` first), shadow-frame cbuffer staging with per-cascade `cCamView=transpose(W2L)` / `cCamProj=I` / anchor, cascade-frustum culling of anchored AABBs (no 1-chunk window), guarded no-op unless the same gate+load+TAA conditions as `splatTerrainDrawDiligent`. 2. **Verify receive + tune bias**: with the caster live, run `scripts/build.sh && ENGINE_HIDDEN_WINDOW=1 VK_ICD_FILENAMES=.../radeon_icd.json ENGINE_AUTOTEST=enter ENGINE_SCREENSHOT=/tmp/terrain_shadow.jpg ./build/c-game/c-game` (and the same with `ENGINE_PBR_BIAS_SCALE` at 1 / 2 / 4) and check: player casts on the ground, terrain casts onto terrain/player (self-shadow visible on slopes), no acne on sunlit terrain (raise bias), no obvious peter-panning (lower bias); also a RenderDoc capture comparing the shadow pass output against the world pass.

4. **(Follow-up, only if step 2 shows visible receive artifacts at distance)**: per-pixel cascade selection + fade-to-lit in the splat PS (approach C) — dispatch separately with its own plan, since the cbuffer layout change ripples through `SplatFrameStaging` and both splat shaders.
